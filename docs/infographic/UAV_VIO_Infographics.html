PITCH  ·  TECHNICAL OVERVIEW

THIRD IRIS

Autonomous air-to-air pursuit when GPS is gone. One camera feeds two minds — a tracking pipeline that locks the target, and a visual-inertial odometry pipeline that keeps the aircraft localized. Every claim below is a measured sim result, not a promise.

01

The Pursuit Pipeline

Five stages, one 10 Hz onboard loop. No GPS anywhere in this chain.

Why it matters: every box is a replaceable module with a proven open-source core — YOLO26 for detection, ByteTrack for association, classic Kalman filtering for prediction. The work is integration and tuning, not research risk.

02

One Camera, Two Minds

Watch the frames flow: every image is consumed twice, by two independent pipelines, at the same instant.

The trick: when GPS dies, the chase does not switch to a degraded mode. The servo loop (top) was never using GPS — it steers off pixel error in the body frame, so VIO position jitter cannot bend the pursuit. The bottom lane only has to keep the aircraft from wandering — and bounded drift is enough for that.

03

What the Servo Sees

Pixel error in, body-frame velocity out. The whole control law fits in one picture.

Why body-frame matters: these three commands reference only the camera image and the airframe itself — no world coordinates. A wrong world-position estimate (the only thing GPS jamming can poison) literally has no input into this loop. That is why tracking survives jamming with 9/11 frames held over ~30 s.

04

The Moment GPS Dies

Measured drift during a 22-second jam, 38 m of flight (vio_test.py).

Bounded vs divergent is the whole game. Pure inertial navigation compounds error every second — unusable within half a minute. Vision pins the estimate to the world: drift grows slowly and stays flat. 4.5 m of self-position error while the body-frame servo (section 03) handles the target means the mission continues.

05

Coasting Through Detector Drop-Outs

Real detectors blink. The track must not. Green = detector hit, red = miss, dashed = Kalman-predicted box.

Coasting: on a missed frame the image-space Kalman filter keeps predicting where the target should be, so the lock never drops and the servo keeps flying a smooth command. When the detector recovers, the filter snaps back to the true box. This is what lets the standoff hold stay smooth through drop-outs instead of jerking or losing the target entirely.

06

Terminal Guidance — Lead, Don't Chase

Proportional navigation aims at where the target will be, not where it is.

Reused, not reinvented: the strike law is classic homing guidance (proportional navigation + a predicted-intercept-point lead term). It runs on the same vision-only target state as tracking — no GPS, no rangefinder. The novelty is the integration: a single camera feeding detection, tracking, localization, and terminal guidance.
